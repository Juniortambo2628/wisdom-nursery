The right tree in the right place gives shade, wood, fruit or income for decades. The wrong one can compete with your crops, drain your water or outgrow its space. Start with what you want the tree to do.

1. Decide what the tree is for

  • Boundaries, shade and windbreaks: Grevillea robusta grows quickly, can be pruned for poles and firewood, and is widely planted alongside crops.
  • Timber, poles and firewood: Blue gum (eucalyptus) and Senna siamea grow fast. Blue gum uses a lot of water, so plant it away from crops, springs and boreholes.
  • Shade and natural pest control: Neem is hardy in dry areas and its leaves are traditionally used as a natural insect repellent.
  • Fruit for home or market: grafted avocado, mango and lemon trees bear fruit years earlier than trees grown from seed and keep the qualities of the parent variety.
  • Compounds and landscaping: palms, Ashoka and cypress give a neat, green look to homes, schools and offices.

2. Match the tree to your land

  • Rainfall: in drier areas choose hardy trees such as Neem and Grevillea, or plan to water through the first dry seasons.
  • Soil: fruit trees need deep, well-drained soil. Waterlogged patches are not suitable for avocado.
  • Space: check the mature size. A mango or avocado tree needs several metres in every direction; palms and Ashoka suit smaller compounds.

3. Plan the spacing

Spacing depends on the tree and your purpose: boundary trees can be planted closer together, while fruit trees need wide spacing for light and harvesting. Tell us your plot size and goal and we will suggest a layout and the number of seedlings you need.

4. Buy healthy, hardened seedlings

  • Sturdy stems and healthy green leaves, without yellowing or spots.
  • Roots that fill the bag without spiralling out of the bottom.
  • Seedlings that have been hardened in the open, not just taken from shade, so they cope with full sun after planting.
  • For fruit trees, a clean, well-healed graft joint.
