Most indoor plants come from the floor of tropical forests, where they grow in dappled light, warm air and loose, damp soil. Copy those conditions at home or in the office and they will thrive for years.

Light: bright, but not direct sun

Place plants near a window where they get plenty of daylight but no hot afternoon sun on the leaves. Direct sun through glass scorches leaves (brown, papery patches). Too little light shows as long, leaning stems and small new leaves.

  • Brighter spots: Bird of Paradise, Strelitzia Nicolai, Coleus.
  • Medium light: Swiss Cheese Plant, Philodendron, Dracaena.
  • Lower light, away from the window: Aglaonema, Broadleaf Lady Palm.

Turn pots a quarter turn every week or two so the plant grows evenly instead of leaning towards the light.

Water: let the top dry out first

More indoor plants die from too much water than too little. Push a finger into the soil: water only when the top 2–3 cm feel dry. Water slowly until a little runs out of the bottom, then empty the saucer after half an hour. See our watering guide for more.

Pots and soil

Always use a pot with drainage holes. If you like a decorative pot without holes, keep the plant in its nursery pot inside it and lift it out to water. Use a light, free-draining potting mix, not garden soil, which packs down hard indoors.

Air and leaves

  • Keep plants away from air-conditioning vents, heaters and draughty doors.
  • Wipe large leaves with a damp cloth every few weeks so they can take in light.
  • Grouping plants together raises the humidity around them, which forest plants like Alocasia and Anthurium enjoy.

Feeding

During the growing months, feed every four weeks with a liquid houseplant fertiliser at half the strength on the label. Skip feeding when the plant is not putting out new leaves.

Common problems

  • Yellow lower leaves, soggy soil: overwatering. Let it dry out and water less often.
  • Brown, crispy leaf tips: dry air or irregular watering.
  • Pale, stretched growth: not enough light. Move it closer to a window.
  • Small insects or sticky leaves: wipe leaves with soapy water and isolate the plant; ask us if it persists.
