There is no single watering timetable that suits every plant: it depends on the plant, the pot, the season and where it stands. These simple checks work better than any calendar.

The finger test

Push a finger into the soil up to the second knuckle.

  • Dry: water now.
  • Slightly damp: check again in a day or two.
  • Wet: leave it. Watering now invites root rot.

You can also lift the pot: a dry pot is noticeably lighter than a freshly watered one. After a few weeks you will know your plants by weight.

How to water

  • Water the soil, not the leaves, until water runs out of the drainage holes.
  • Empty saucers after 30 minutes; roots should never sit in water.
  • Water in the morning so leaves and soil surface dry during the day.
  • Use water at room temperature. Rainwater is ideal if you can collect it.

Indoor plants

Most of our indoor plants need water about once a week in warm months and less in cooler months. Large plants in big pots dry out more slowly than small ones. Plants with thick, fleshy leaves or stems, such as Felt Bush, store water and need less.

Seedlings and young trees outdoors

Young trees have small root systems and cannot reach deep water yet.

  • First two weeks after planting: water every two to three days if it does not rain.
  • First dry season: water deeply once or twice a week (about 10–20 litres per tree) rather than a little every day. Deep watering grows deep roots.
  • A ring of soil around the tree (a small basin) holds the water where the roots are.
  • Mulch with dry grass or leaves to keep the soil cool and slow evaporation, keeping it a hand's width from the stem.

Signs to watch for

  • Too much water: yellowing leaves, soft stems, soil that stays wet, a sour smell.
  • Too little water: drooping leaves that recover after watering, dry crispy edges, soil pulling away from the pot.
