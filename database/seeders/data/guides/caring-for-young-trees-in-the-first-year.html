The first year decides whether a young tree survives. A little care at the right times gets it through the dry season and growing strongly by the next rains.

Water deeply, not often

  • First two weeks: water every two to three days if there is no rain.
  • After that: water deeply once or twice a week in dry weather, about 10–20 litres per tree, poured slowly into the basin around the stem.
  • Deep watering trains roots to grow downwards, where the soil stays moist longer.

More detail in our watering guide.

Mulch and weed

Keep a circle about one metre wide around each tree free of weeds and grass, which compete for water. Cover it with a layer of dry grass, leaves or crop residue, kept a hand's width from the stem. Mulch keeps the soil cool and moist and slowly feeds it.

Protect from animals and pests

  • Keep fences and cages in good repair: one visit from a goat can strip a young tree.
  • Check the base of the stem for termites, especially in dry months, and ask us for advice if you see damage.
  • Look under leaves for insects, and remove badly damaged leaves.

Grafted fruit trees

  • Rub off any shoots growing from below the graft joint as soon as you see them. They come from the rootstock and will not give the variety of fruit you bought.
  • Remove flowers and small fruit in the first year or two so the tree puts its energy into roots and branches.
  • Feed with well-rotted manure or compost spread around the basin at the start of the rains.

Staking and pruning

Check ties on staked trees every few weeks and loosen them so they do not cut into the stem. Remove stakes once the tree stands firmly on its own. Apart from removing damaged or crossing branches, leave major pruning until the tree is established.

Replace losses early

Even with good care a few seedlings may not survive. Replace them early in the next rains so your plot fills in evenly. We keep seedlings ready through the year.
