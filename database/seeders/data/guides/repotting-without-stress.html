Repotting gives a growing plant fresh soil and room for its roots. Done gently and at the right time, the plant hardly notices.

When to repot

  • Roots are growing out of the drainage holes or circling the inside of the pot.
  • Water runs straight through without soaking in.
  • The plant dries out within a day or two of watering.
  • It has been in the same soil for two years or more.

Repot when the plant is actively growing, and avoid repotting a plant that is already stressed or has just been moved.

What you need

  • A pot only 3–5 cm wider than the current one, with drainage holes. A much bigger pot holds too much wet soil.
  • Fresh, free-draining potting mix.
  • Gloves, newspaper and a watering can.

Step by step

  1. Water the plant the day before so the root ball holds together.
  2. Turn the pot on its side, support the base of the stems and ease the plant out. Tap or squeeze a plastic pot to loosen it; never pull by the stems.
  3. Gently loosen circling roots with your fingers. Trim any that are black and mushy.
  4. Put a layer of fresh mix in the new pot so the top of the root ball sits about 2 cm below the rim.
  5. Set the plant in the centre, fill around it with mix and firm lightly. Keep the stem at the same depth as before.
  6. Water well and let it drain.
  7. Keep the plant out of strong light for a week while it settles, and do not feed it for about a month.

Plants grown in nursery bags

Seedlings and young trees from the nursery come in polythene bags. When you plant them, always cut and remove the bag completely: roots cannot grow through it. See how to plant a tree seedling.
