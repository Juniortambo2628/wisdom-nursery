A well-planted seedling grows faster and survives its first dry season far more often. The work you put into the planting hole pays back for years.

When to plant

Plant at the start of the rains, once the soil is moist to a depth of about 30 cm, so the young roots have a whole rainy season to establish. If you can water regularly, you can plant at other times too.

Choosing the spot

  • Check how big the tree will grow and leave enough space from buildings, water pipes, boreholes and power lines.
  • Most trees need full sun.
  • Fruit trees need well-drained soil; avocados in particular do not tolerate waterlogged ground.

Digging the hole

  • Tree seedlings (Grevillea, Neem, Senna): about 30 cm wide and 30 cm deep.
  • Grafted fruit trees (avocado, mango, lemon): about 60 cm wide and 60 cm deep.

Keep the dark topsoil in one pile and the lighter subsoil in another. Mix the topsoil with well-rotted manure or compost (about one part manure to two parts topsoil). Never use fresh manure, which burns roots.

Planting

  1. Water the seedling in its bag an hour before planting.
  2. Half-fill the hole with the topsoil and manure mix.
  3. Cut the polythene bag down the side and remove it completely without breaking the soil around the roots.
  4. Set the seedling so the top of its soil is level with the ground. On grafted trees, keep the graft joint (the bump on the stem) well above the soil.
  5. Fill around it with the remaining mix and firm gently with your hands to remove air pockets.
  6. Make a small basin of soil around the tree to hold water, and water with 10–20 litres.
  7. Mulch with dry grass, keeping it a hand's width away from the stem.

Protect it

  • Fence or cage young trees where goats, cattle or other animals graze.
  • Tie taller seedlings loosely to a stake in windy places.
  • Check for termites around the stem during the first months.
